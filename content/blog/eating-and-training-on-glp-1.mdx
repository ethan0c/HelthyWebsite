"I'm barely hungry anymore, so do I still need to worry about protein?" If you're on semaglutide (Ozempic, Wegovy) or tirzepatide (Mounjaro, Zepbound), yes. More than before, if anything.

The short answer: make protein and lifting the two things you don't skip. Aim for at least **1.6 g of protein per kg of your goal weight a day** (about 0.7 g per lb), eaten first at every meal, and do **2 or 3 full-body strength sessions a week**. For someone aiming to weigh 175 lb (79 kg), that's about 125 g of protein a day, or roughly 20–25 g across five or six small meals.

These drugs make eating less easy. They don't decide what the weight you lose is made of. That part is still up to you.

## Why you lose muscle on a GLP-1 (and on any big diet)

A GLP-1 medication doesn't dissolve muscle. What it does is turn down appetite hard enough that many people end up in a large, fast calorie deficit, often without trying. And any large, fast deficit costs some lean mass, whether it comes from a drug, a crash diet or surgery.

When energy is short, your body breaks down some protein for fuel, and muscle is the biggest store it has. A smaller body also needs less muscle to carry around, so without a reason to keep it (lifting), it doesn't.

The old rule of thumb from diet research is that roughly a quarter of the weight people lose is lean mass, and that the share goes up when the deficit is big, protein is low and nobody's lifting. "Barely hungry" usually takes care of the first two on its own.

## What the GLP-1 trials found about muscle loss

The two headline trials were STEP 1 (semaglutide, about 2,000 adults, 68 weeks) and SURMOUNT-1 (tirzepatide, about 2,500 adults, 72 weeks). Both produced far more weight loss than placebo, and both ran body scans (DXA, the kind used for bone density) on a small subgroup to see what that weight was made of.

Across these and similar trials, lean mass made up somewhere between roughly a quarter and two-fifths of the weight lost, with the semaglutide subgroup toward the top of that range and the tirzepatide subgroup toward the bottom. Fat still made up most of the loss, and because fat fell faster than lean mass, people's lean mass as a share of their body weight actually went up.

There's real debate about what those numbers mean:

- **The subgroups were small.** Each scan group was well under 200 people.
- **"Lean mass" isn't the same as muscle.** DXA counts everything that isn't fat or bone, including water, the glycogen stored with it, and organs like the liver, which shrinks as it loses fat. Some of that loss isn't muscle at all.
- **Nobody was asked to lift or hit a protein target.** Participants got standard diet and activity counseling, so the trials show what happens by default, not what's possible.
- **The share is roughly in line with other big weight losses.** Researchers who've compared them generally find a similar split with diet-only weight loss of a similar size, and with bariatric surgery.

Here's our honest read: nobody knows yet exactly how much real muscle people lose on these drugs, and anyone quoting one scary percentage as fact is overselling it. But "some, unless you do something about it" is a safe bet, and the fix is the same either way.

## How much protein do you need on a GLP-1?

Stay with the numbers from our guide to [how much protein you need to build muscle](/blog/how-much-protein-to-build-muscle): 1.6 g/kg is the point where most of the benefit is in, and about 2.2 g/kg is a sensible ceiling. When you're dieting, the top half of that range helps a little more.

Two adjustments for people on these drugs.

First, **base it on your goal weight**, not your current one. If you have a lot of fat to lose, a target based on today's weight gives you a number you physically can't eat, and fat tissue doesn't need feeding the way muscle does.

Second, **treat 1.6 g/kg as the floor you actually hit, not the low end you drift under.** We'd rather you land on 1.6 every day than aim for 2.2 and manage 1.1 because each meal is four bites. If your appetite allows more, go up toward 1.8–2.2.

The caveat: the big drug trials didn't set or test protein targets, so these numbers are borrowed from general dieting and training research. (If you have kidney disease, which is common alongside type 2 diabetes, ask your doctor before raising protein. The advice for you is different.)

### A worked example

Say you weigh 240 lb (109 kg) and your goal is 175 lb (79 kg).

- Based on current weight: 1.6 × 109 = about 174 g a day. On a suppressed appetite, that's unrealistic.
- Based on goal weight: 1.6 × 79 = about **126 g a day**. Up to about 145 g (1.8 g/kg) on good days.

Split over six small eating times, 126 g is about 21 g each. Here's one way it looks:

1. **Breakfast:** 200 g (7 oz) plain Greek yogurt, about 20 g.
2. **Mid-morning:** a ready-to-drink protein shake, about 25 g.
3. **Lunch:** 3 oz (85 g) canned tuna on a few crackers, about 20 g.
4. **Afternoon:** 2 hard-boiled eggs and a stick of string cheese, about 19 g.
5. **Dinner:** 3 oz (85 g) chicken breast with a few bites of rice and vegetables, about 26 g.
6. **Evening:** ½ cup (113 g) cottage cheese, about 13 g.

That's about 123 g, close enough. None of those portions would count as a big meal, which is the point.

### High-protein foods that are easy to eat

When you're full after a few bites, the best foods pack a lot of protein into a small, soft or drinkable volume. Protein figures are approximate and brands vary, so check your labels.

| Food | Serving | Protein (approx.) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Protein shake (ready to drink) | 1 bottle | 20–30 g |
| Plain Greek yogurt or skyr | 170 g (6 oz) | 15–18 g |
| Cottage cheese | ½ cup (113 g) | 12–14 g |
| Chicken breast, cooked | 3 oz (85 g) | about 26 g |
| Canned tuna or chicken | 3 oz (85 g), drained | 18–22 g |
| White fish (cod), cooked | 3 oz (85 g) | about 19 g |
| Eggs | 2 large | about 12 g |
| Firm tofu | 100 g (3.5 oz) | 12–15 g |
| String cheese | 1 stick | 6–8 g |
| Milk (dairy or soy) | 1 cup (240 ml) | 7–8 g |

We'd lean on shakes, yogurt and cottage cheese more than you might on a normal diet. Liquids and soft foods are generally easier to get down when you're queasy, and a 25 g shake beats a chicken breast you only manage a third of.

## Why strength training matters more on a GLP-1

Protein gives your body the material to keep muscle. Lifting gives it the reason.

The best evidence comes from diet studies rather than the drug trials. In a 2017 trial in the *New England Journal of Medicine*, Dennis Villareal and colleagues assigned 160 older adults with obesity either to a control group or to six months of a calorie-reduced diet plus aerobic exercise, resistance training, or both. The three diet groups lost similar amounts of weight, but the two that lifted lost noticeably less lean mass than the cardio-only group. The limits: these were adults over 65, nobody was on a GLP-1, and six months is short.

If you only have time for one kind of exercise, we'd pick lifting over cardio. Cardio is good for your heart and your mood, but it does very little to tell your body to keep muscle during a deficit, and the drug is already handling the calorie side. Walking on top is a great addition, not a replacement.

### A simple 2–3 day starting plan

Alternate two full-body workouts, with at least a day off between sessions. On two days a week, do A then B. On three, go A, B, A one week and B, A, B the next.

**Workout A**

- [Goblet squat](/exercises/goblet-squat): 2–3 sets of 8–12
- [Dumbbell bench press](/exercises/dumbbell-bench-press): 2–3 sets of 8–12
- [Dumbbell row](/exercises/dumbbell-row): 2–3 sets of 10–12 per arm
- [Hip thrust](/exercises/hip-thrust): 2–3 sets of 10–12

**Workout B**

- [Leg press](/exercises/leg-press): 2–3 sets of 10–12
- [Push-up](/exercises/push-up) (hands on a bench if needed): 2–3 sets of 6–12
- [Lat pulldown](/exercises/lat-pulldown): 2–3 sets of 10–12
- [Romanian deadlift](/exercises/romanian-deadlift): 2–3 sets of 8–10

Start with 2 sets and pick weights you could lift 2 or 3 more times at the end of each set. When you hit the top of the rep range on every set, go up a little. That's [progressive overload](/blog/progressive-overload) in its simplest form.

A few adjustments that matter more when you're eating very little:

- **Eat something with protein a couple of hours before you train.** Lifting on an empty stomach after a day of barely eating is when people feel shaky.
- **Stop the set if you feel dizzy or lightheaded,** sit down and drink something. Burning muscles are normal effort; dizziness and sharp joint pain are not.
- **Holding your strength counts as winning.** Steady lifts while the scale drops are a good sign you're keeping muscle.

Once this feels easy and you want more, a split like [push/pull/legs](/blog/push-pull-legs-split) is a natural next step.

## How to eat when you're nauseous or full fast

Nausea, fullness after a few bites and constipation are common, especially early on and after dose changes. What usually helps:

- **Protein first.** Think of your appetite as a carry-on bag instead of a suitcase: pack the essentials before anything else. Eat the yogurt, eggs or chicken before the toast.
- **Eat small, often, and stop at the first sign of fullness.** Eating past it is when nausea tends to hit.
- **Go easy on greasy, fried and very rich food.** High-fat meals are a common trigger, and plain, cool foods are often easier.
- **Sip fluids through the day.** Small amounts between meals are easier than a big glass with food, and dehydration makes everything worse.
- **Add fiber gradually for constipation.** Fruit, oats, beans and vegetables help, along with fluids and a daily walk. Fiber is filling, though, so it comes after protein, not instead of it.

Call your prescriber promptly if you're vomiting and can't keep fluids down, have severe stomach pain that won't settle (especially if it spreads to your back), or notice signs of dehydration like dizziness and very little urine.

## Tracking protein and lifts when you're eating very little

On a normal diet, missing 20 g of protein at lunch is a small dent. When your whole day is six small portions, one skipped snack can be a sixth of your intake, and it's easy to run at 70 g for weeks while feeling like you're doing fine.

Our view: while the drug has your appetite turned down, track protein and let calories take care of themselves. The usual problem on these medications isn't eating too much, it's eating too little of the right thing. Log what you eat for a couple of weeks, find the gaps ("breakfast is coffee") and fix those. Log your lifts too. If your strength slides for several weeks in a row, protein or overall food has usually dropped too low, and it shows in the logbook before the mirror. If the scale has stalled instead, our guide on [why you're not losing weight](/blog/why-am-i-not-losing-weight) covers the usual culprits.

## What happens when you stop a GLP-1

Weight regain is common when these drugs stop. In the STEP 1 extension, researchers followed a subgroup of participants for a year after both semaglutide and the trial's lifestyle program ended. On average they regained about two-thirds of the weight they'd lost. A later tirzepatide trial, SURMOUNT-4, found the same pattern: people switched to placebo regained much of their loss, while those who stayed on the drug kept losing. The limits: the extension was a subgroup of a few hundred people, and in STEP 1 the support program stopped at the same time as the drug, so you can't fully separate the two.

Whether and when to change or stop your medication is a decision to make with your prescriber, following their guidance, not something to base on a blog post.

What you can control is what you'll have built by then. Muscle you keep now is muscle you don't have to rebuild, and a protein habit and a lifting routine will still be there when your appetite comes back. We'll be honest that large trials haven't properly tested whether those habits blunt the regain, so we can't promise they will. They're still the best thing you can do with the time. If your intake does change, the [TDEE calculator](/tools/tdee-calculator) will give you a starting maintenance number to plan around.

If you're on one of these drugs right now, here's the default: work out your protein target from your goal weight with the [protein calculator](/tools/protein-calculator), hit 1.6 g/kg of it every day before worrying about anything else, and lift 2 or 3 times a week. Go higher on protein only if your appetite genuinely has room; on a day when it doesn't, a shake and a yogurt still count.
