## The free options and their limits

### Cronometer for detailed nutrition tracking

Cronometer Basic includes unlimited barcode scans, calorie and macro tracking, vitamins and minerals, and custom meals and recipes. It suits someone who wants nutrient detail alongside a daily calorie total.

Its free nutrition reports cover a seven-day window. That reporting limit doesn't establish a daily food-entry or barcode-scan cap. Cronometer explicitly lists unlimited scanner use in its [Basic plan](https://cronometer.com/features/free-nutrition-tracking-app.html).

### MyNetDiary for an ad-free food diary

MyNetDiary includes unlimited barcode scanning, calorie and macro tracking, and custom foods and recipes in its [free plan](https://www.mynetdiary.com/free-calorie-tracker.html). It also keeps that plan ad-free.

Those features make it a useful starting point if you mainly want to log meals without paying for a scanner. Test a few products you buy regularly, then check how easily you can change the serving weight and find those foods again.

### Helthy for food and workout logging together

[Helthy](/calorie-tracker) combines calorie and macro tracking with a workout log for sets, reps and weights. The free plan includes **unlimited barcode scans**, food logging through search, saved meals and quick add, plus unlimited workout logging.

**Only photo scans count toward the scan allowance.** Scanning a packaged food's barcode doesn't use that allowance or require Premium. Premium adds unlimited photo scanning, coaching, generated workout routines and full history.

Helthy is our pick if you want to scan packaged foods throughout the day and track your lifting in the same app. You can keep using the barcode scanner for free even after you reach your photo-scan limit.

## How accurate is barcode calorie tracking?

A barcode identifies a product so an app can look up its food entry. The result still depends on the database entry and the amount you log. A successful scan alone doesn't verify either one.

Before saving a food for the first time, check three things:

1. **The product matches.** Check the brand, flavor and version.
2. **The nutrition values match the package.** Compare calories and macros using the same serving size or the same weight.
3. **The quantity matches what you ate.** A package can contain several servings.

The [FDA's guide to nutrition labels](https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-facts-label/how-understand-and-use-nutrition-facts-label) explains how calories and nutrients scale with the number of servings you eat.

### A serving-size example

Suppose a cereal label lists **160 calories and 6 g of protein per 40 g serving**. You weigh out 60 g. That's 1.5 servings, so your entry should show **240 calories and 9 g of protein**.

## Barcode scanning for Indian groceries and home cooking

For Indian packaged foods, choose an app by testing your own groceries. A database's total size doesn't tell you whether it contains your usual milk, snacks, atta or protein powder. We haven't tested a representative set of Indian products, so we can't rank these apps by regional coverage.

If a product is missing, create a custom food from its nutrition panel. Check whether the panel gives values per serving or per 100 g, and use the matching unit in the app. Save it under a name you'll recognize next time.

For homemade dal, curry or rice dishes, a saved recipe is more useful than a barcode. Record the ingredients, including cooking oil, and divide the batch into portions. A photo can help identify the foods, but you still need to review the ingredients and quantities it suggests.

## What to check before choosing an app

Try the free plan with a normal day's meals. Include a packaged food, a food without a barcode and a meal you cook often. Check whether you can correct an entry, change its weight and reuse it the next day.

Also check the features you'll use after logging: daily protein totals, saved recipes and access to past meals. Scanner access, report history and custom-food limits are separate features. A plan can be generous in one area and restrictive in another.

Once you've chosen a tracker, set calorie and protein targets you can review against your progress. Our guides to [calorie targets for weight loss](/blog/how-many-calories-to-lose-weight) and [protein for muscle growth](/blog/how-much-protein-to-build-muscle) explain where to start.
