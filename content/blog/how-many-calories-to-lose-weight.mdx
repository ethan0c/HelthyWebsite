The short answer: eat a few hundred calories below what you burn each day, keep your protein high, and adjust based on what the scale does over two to three weeks. The details below cover how to find each of those numbers.

## Step 1: Find your maintenance calories (TDEE)

Your total daily energy expenditure (TDEE) is how many calories you burn in a day. It's made of four parts:

- **BMR**, the energy your body uses at complete rest. For most people this is the biggest share.
- **Everyday movement** (NEAT): walking, standing, fidgeting, chores.
- **Exercise**: your workouts.
- **Digestion** (the thermic effect of food), roughly 10% of what you eat.

Eat at your TDEE and your weight stays roughly the same. You can estimate it in a few seconds with our [TDEE calculator](/tools/tdee-calculator), which uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, or Katch-McArdle if you know your body fat percentage.

Treat that number as a starting guess. Formulas are typically within about 10% for most people, which can mean a couple of hundred calories either way.

## Step 2: Choose your deficit

A kilogram of body fat stores roughly 7,700 calories (about 3,500 per pound). So a daily deficit of around 550 calories works out to about 0.5 kg (1.1 lb) of fat loss a week.

A good rule is to aim to lose **0.5–1% of your bodyweight a week**:

| Bodyweight | 0.5% a week | 1% a week |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 140 lb (64 kg) | 0.7 lb | 1.4 lb |
| 180 lb (82 kg) | 0.9 lb | 1.8 lb |
| 220 lb (100 kg) | 1.1 lb | 2.2 lb |

Faster isn't better. Big deficits make you hungrier, drain your training and make it harder to keep muscle. Most people don't stick with them past a few weeks.

It's also worth setting a floor. As a general guide, don't go below about 1,500 calories a day for men or 1,200 for women without medical supervision.

## Step 3: Set your protein first

When you eat less, your body is more willing to break down muscle for energy. Eating plenty of protein and lifting weights are the two best ways to prevent that.

A solid target while dieting is about **0.85 g of protein per pound of bodyweight** (about 1.9 g per kg), or per pound of your goal weight if you have a lot to lose. Protein also keeps you fuller than carbs or fat for the same calories, which makes a deficit easier to live with. Use the [protein calculator](/tools/protein-calculator) to get your number and split it across meals.

Once protein is set, how you divide the rest between carbs and fat matters much less than your total calories. Pick the split you can stick to; the [macro calculator](/tools/macro-calculator) will do the math.

## Step 4: Track honestly for two weeks

This is where most plans fall apart. People are consistently poor at estimating how much they eat, and underestimates of several hundred calories a day are common. That's enough to wipe out the whole deficit.

You don't need to track forever, but you do need to track accurately at the start. A few tips:

- **Log as you eat**, not at the end of the day.
- **Weigh foods at home** for the first week or two. You'll quickly learn what 100 g of rice or a tablespoon of oil actually looks like.
- **Don't skip the extras**: cooking oil, sauces, drinks and bites while cooking add up fast.
- **Use the fastest method for each meal.** In [Helthy](/calorie-tracker) that's usually a barcode scan for packaged food, a photo for a plated meal, and search or quick-add for everything else.

## Step 5: Adjust from real data

Your daily weight will bounce around by a kilogram or more because of water, salt, carbs and digestion. Ignore single weigh-ins and watch the weekly average.

After two to three weeks, compare your average weekly change with your target:

- **Losing about as planned:** change nothing.
- **Not losing:** your real TDEE is lower than the estimate, or your logging is missing something. Tighten up the logging first, then drop about 150–200 calories a day.
- **Losing much faster than planned:** add about 150–200 calories back, especially if training is suffering.

Expect to adjust again as you get lighter, because a smaller body burns fewer calories.

## The takeaway

Start from an estimate, keep the deficit moderate, eat plenty of protein, track accurately and let your weekly trend tell you what to change. The number that matters isn't what a calculator says on day one; it's what keeps your weight moving at the rate you want.
